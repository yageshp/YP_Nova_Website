What business process automation covers

Business process automation (BPA) uses software to carry out the predictable parts of a process: moving a request to the next person, notifying the right people, updating related records and compiling reports. People keep the parts that need judgement; the system handles the movement and the record-keeping.

A practical way to start is described in our guide to business workflow automation.

Approval workflow automation

Purchase requests, permits, leave, expenses and document sign-offs are routed through defined approval levels. Each approver sees what is waiting for them, decisions are recorded with time and comments, and rejected items return to the requester with the reason. See a delivered example in the work permit management system.

Reporting automation

Reports that are assembled by hand every week or month are generated from source data on a schedule or on demand. This is how our MIS produces meter-reading and operational reports directly from the sheets site teams already maintain.

Data synchronisation

When the same information lives in more than one place — a spreadsheet and an application, or two systems — synchronisation keeps them consistent automatically, removing re-typing and the errors it introduces.

Notification automation

Status changes trigger the right alert to the right person: a new complaint notifies the help-desk, an approved permit notifies the requester, an overdue item escalates to a supervisor. Notifications can be sent by email or in-app, and other channels are scoped per project.

Document workflows

Forms, attachments and generated documents travel with the request they belong to, with versions and approvals kept together. Where documents arrive as unstructured text, AI-assisted extraction can prepare structured data for review.

API integrations

Automation often depends on connecting systems. We build REST API integrations between your business application and tools such as Google Sheets, accounting software or reporting platforms, provided those systems offer suitable access.

AI workflow automation

Rule-based automation handles structured steps. Where a step involves reading emails or documents, an AI component can classify, extract or summarise the content and pass it into the workflow for a person to confirm. We explain the difference in agentic AI development.

Frequently asked questions

What is business process automation?

It is the use of software to carry out the predictable steps of a business process — routing approvals, sending notifications, updating records and producing reports — so people spend their time on decisions rather than on moving information around.

Which processes should we automate first?

Start with a process that is repeated often, follows clear rules, involves hand-offs between people and is already well understood — for example purchase approvals, permit approvals or a weekly report.

Do we need to replace our existing software?

Not necessarily. Automation can connect existing tools through APIs or work alongside spreadsheets your teams already use. We recommend replacing a tool only when it is the source of the problem.

Can automation include AI?

Yes, for steps involving unstructured text or documents. AI prepares the result and a person confirms it before it affects records, payments or customers.

How do we know automation is working?

Every automated step is logged, and dashboards show volumes, pending items and turnaround times, so you can see where work is flowing and where it is stuck.
